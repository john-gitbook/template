# History of NVIDIA

## History of NVIDIA

NVIDIA began as a graphics-chip company and became a central provider of accelerated computing hardware. Its graphics processing units, or GPUs, changed video games, professional visualization, scientific computing, and artificial intelligence.

### Founding and early graphics

Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky, and Curtis Priem founded NVIDIA in 1993. The company focused on graphics hardware for personal computers. At the time, 3D graphics were becoming important for games and visual applications.

NVIDIA’s early products faced intense competition and technical challenges. The company refined its design approach and released the RIVA series during the late 1990s. These products helped establish NVIDIA as a major graphics supplier.

In 1999, NVIDIA introduced the GeForce 256. The company described it as the first GPU, a processor designed to accelerate graphics calculations. The name became a long-running consumer brand.

### Graphics leadership

NVIDIA’s GPUs became important for PC gaming. Better graphics hardware enabled more detailed worlds, lighting effects, animation, and higher display resolutions. Competition with ATI, later acquired by AMD, drove rapid improvements.

The company also developed Quadro products for professional visualization. These products served designers, engineers, animators, and researchers who needed reliable graphics performance for specialized software.

NVIDIA entered the console market through graphics technology used in systems such as the original Xbox. It continued to work with gaming hardware partners while expanding its own PC graphics business.

### CUDA and accelerated computing

In 2006, NVIDIA introduced CUDA, a programming platform for using GPUs beyond graphics. Developers could apply parallel GPU processing to scientific simulations, data analysis, engineering workloads, and machine learning.

CUDA became a strategic asset. It linked NVIDIA’s hardware to a software ecosystem of libraries, tools, developers, and research institutions. This made GPUs more useful for computing tasks that could run in parallel.

Deep-learning researchers adopted GPUs because training neural networks requires large amounts of matrix computation. NVIDIA hardware and CUDA software became widely used in AI research and production systems.

### Data centers and AI

As AI workloads expanded, NVIDIA built products for data centers. Its Tesla and later data-center GPU lines supported training and inference workloads. The company also developed networking and systems technologies for large-scale computing.

NVIDIA acquired Mellanox in 2020. The acquisition added high-speed networking products used in data centers and supercomputers. It strengthened NVIDIA’s ability to offer integrated AI infrastructure.

The company’s accelerated-computing strategy now spans chips, networking, systems, developer software, and cloud services. Its products are used by universities, cloud providers, enterprises, and research labs.

### Current role

NVIDIA also develops technologies for autonomous vehicles, robotics, digital twins, and edge computing. Its platforms combine hardware with software frameworks for specific industries.

The company’s influence has grown with demand for generative AI. Supply constraints, export controls, competition, energy use, and dependence on a few advanced manufacturers shape its operating environment. NVIDIA remains closely associated with the technical infrastructure behind modern AI.
