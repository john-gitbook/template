# History of Microsoft

## History of Microsoft

Microsoft began as a small software company and became a defining force in personal computing. Its products helped establish the software standards used in offices, homes, schools, and data centers worldwide.

### Founding and early software

Bill Gates and Paul Allen founded Microsoft in 1975. The company’s original name, Micro-Soft, combined “microcomputer” and “software.” Its first major work was a version of the BASIC programming language for the Altair 8800.

The Altair helped launch the hobbyist microcomputer market. Gates and Allen saw that these machines needed software, not only hardware. Their BASIC interpreter gave users a practical way to write and run programs.

Microsoft moved to Bellevue, Washington, and later established its headquarters in Redmond. It focused on language tools and operating-system software for the growing computer industry.

### IBM PC and MS-DOS

In 1980, IBM selected Microsoft to provide an operating system for the IBM Personal Computer. Microsoft licensed and adapted software that became MS-DOS. The IBM PC launched in 1981.

A key part of Microsoft’s agreement was the right to license MS-DOS to other hardware manufacturers. As companies built IBM-compatible PCs, Microsoft’s operating system became the standard across a rapidly expanding market.

Microsoft’s position came from software distribution rather than computer manufacturing. The company could serve many PC makers at once. This model helped it scale quickly.

### Windows and Office

Microsoft introduced Windows in 1985. Early versions provided a graphical environment on top of MS-DOS. Windows gained broad adoption with Windows 3.0 in 1990 and Windows 3.1 in 1992.

Windows 95 was a major turning point. It introduced the Start menu, taskbar, long file names, and a more integrated consumer interface. The release became a major cultural event for personal computing.

Microsoft also built a productivity suite around Word, Excel, and PowerPoint. Office became widely used in businesses and schools. The combination of Windows and Office strengthened Microsoft’s influence over desktop computing.

### Internet, gaming, and enterprise software

During the 1990s, Microsoft expanded into web technologies, server software, and enterprise tools. Internet Explorer became part of Windows, creating a major antitrust dispute in the United States.

Microsoft entered the game-console market with Xbox in 2001. Xbox Live brought online multiplayer services to a broader console audience. Xbox later expanded through Game Pass, cloud gaming, studios, and acquisitions.

The company’s enterprise business grew through Windows Server, SQL Server, developer tools, and business applications. These products became important sources of long-term customer relationships.

### Leadership changes and cloud computing

Bill Gates stepped down as chief executive in 2000. Steve Ballmer succeeded him and led Microsoft through major growth in enterprise software, Xbox, and cloud infrastructure. The company also faced difficult efforts in mobile computing and online services.

Satya Nadella became chief executive in 2014. His leadership emphasized cloud services, subscription software, open-source collaboration, and developer platforms. Microsoft Azure became one of the company’s central businesses.

Microsoft 365 shifted Office toward a subscription model. GitHub, acquired in 2018, extended Microsoft’s role in software development. LinkedIn, acquired in 2016, added a large professional-network platform.

### AI and the modern company

Microsoft has invested heavily in artificial intelligence, including a major partnership with OpenAI. It has added AI features across Azure, Microsoft 365, GitHub, Windows, and security products.

Today, Microsoft operates across cloud computing, productivity software, operating systems, gaming, business applications, devices, and AI. Its history shows how a company built on programming languages and PC software adapted to the internet and cloud eras.
